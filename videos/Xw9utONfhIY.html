Core thesis

Code correctness and product correctness are different. Moor describes a development process that carries the reasons for a change through intake, implementation, review, and later instruction updates. The aim is fewer repeated product mistakes, not a larger prompt for its own sake.

Big ideas and timestamped moments

At 6:39, a deliberately overcomplicated settings image illustrates how individually plausible changes can damage a product. At 15:56 to 17:27, the examples become concrete: database transactions held open, previously rejected iconography, and incorrect ordering of team and workspace labels. These are internal examples, not a controlled comparison of review products.

The daily lesson extraction at 19:00 is useful only if lessons remain current. A past decision may cease to apply. The talk acknowledges accumulation but does not describe a full process for superseding old decisions.

Verdict

Agree, high confidence, that code alone can omit product intent. The captions and retained review frame provide a specific example of prior incident context. The useful action is retaining decision provenance with an issue and carrying it into review.

Mixed, moderate confidence, on the implied gains from more agent-mediated context. Retrieval can supply relevant history or stale instructions. The product documentation confirms capabilities, but causal improvement remains unmeasured here. A better evaluation would check whether the added context changes correct decisions rather than merely increasing review comments.

Mixed, low confidence, on extrapolating falling model costs and longer task horizons into inevitable autonomous development. The talk itself questions how much long-running output ships. Those forecasts are not needed to justify preserving product decisions now.

Practical takeaways / recommended workflow

Assistant proposal: store a decision’s rationale, owner, date, and superseded status with the issue. Give reviewers only relevant retrieved records and ask them to cite the record behind a concern. Periodically test the system against deliberate reversals so old guidance does not veto new approved behavior. These are proposed safeguards, not the exact Linear automation.

My read / why it matters

The strongest idea is preserving why a change exists. The unresolved work is deciding when a historical reason still applies.
